# PHR Study Resources

Material and resources for pursuing the **Professional in Human Resources (PHR®)** credential — built from HRCI's current **PHR Exam Content Outline (2024)**, current as of September 2026.

## A note on currency

The 2024 ECO is the live outline for PHR through 2026 — HRCI hasn't issued a newer version. It reorganized the exam from its older structure into **7 functional areas**, most notably adding a standalone **Employee Engagement** functional area (17% — the second-heaviest on the exam) that didn't exist as its own category before. If a study resource lists only 5 or 6 functional areas, or doesn't mention Employee Engagement separately from Employee & Labor Relations, it's using a pre-2024 outline.

## Overview

- **Who is it for?** PHR is HRCI's operational-level, U.S.-employment-law-focused HR credential — for HR generalists, specialists, managers, and business partners who apply HR knowledge in day-to-day practice. It's a **knowledge- and experience-based** exam: expect "does this law apply, and what's the correct operational response" scenario framing throughout.
- **PHR vs. SHRM-CP**: both are operational-level HR credentials, but PHR is built entirely around **U.S. federal employment law compliance** as its organizing spine, while SHRM-CP (see the separate SHRM-CP guide in this collection) is built around SHRM's broader behavioral-competency framework. Many HR professionals hold both.
- **Eligibility** (tiered by education, HRCI's standard structure): roughly 1 year of professional-level HR experience with a master's degree, 2 years with a bachelor's degree, or 4 years without a degree — confirm exact current requirements on HRCI's site before applying, as eligibility rules are reviewed periodically.
- **Exam format**: 175 questions (150 scored + 25 unscored pretest items), 3-hour time limit. Scaled score from 100–700; **500 required to pass** (a "did not pass" result reports 100–499, so the reported number does indicate how close you came).
- **Functional area weights (2024 ECO):**

| Functional Area | Weight |
|---|---|
| Business Management | 14% |
| Workforce Planning & Talent Acquisition | 14% |
| Learning & Development | 10% |
| Total Rewards | 15% |
| Employee Engagement | 17% |
| Employee & Labor Relations | 20% |
| HR Information Management | 10% |

- **Test-taking strategies**
  - Employee & Labor Relations (20%) and Employee Engagement (17%) together are more than a third of the exam — prioritize these, along with Total Rewards (15%), before the smaller-weighted areas
  - "Employment laws change constantly" is HRCI's own stated caveat in the ECO — candidates are responsible for knowing the law as it stands *at the time of their exam*, not necessarily what a slightly older study guide describes; verify currency of any legal reference material you use
  - PHR scenarios test correct operational application (e.g., "is this FMLA-eligible?") rather than strategic policy design — if you're torn between an operationally correct answer and a strategically appealing one, choose the operationally correct one
